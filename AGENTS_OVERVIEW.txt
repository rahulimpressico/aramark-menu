================================================================================
              ARAMARK GRILL STATION — WHAT EACH AGENT DOES
================================================================================

Think of this system like a team of checkers. Each checker (agent) has one
specific job. They all look at the weekly menu and check different things.
At the end, one person writes up the final report.

Here is what each checker does, in simple words.

================================================================================
AGENT 1 — Menu Loader
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Opens and reads the menu data file. Then cuts it down so that only the
  items for the right restaurant station (e.g. Grill Station) and the right
  meal time (e.g. Lunch) are included. All other checkers work on this
  filtered-down menu.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — Open the menu data file.
  Step 2 — Remove everything that does not belong to the requested station.
  Step 3 — Remove everything that is not in the requested meal period.
  Step 4 — Hand the clean, smaller menu to all the other agents.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  Imagine a full school timetable for the whole school.
  This agent cuts it down to just "Class 5B, Monday".

================================================================================
AGENT 2 — Cost & Ingredient Summary
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Looks at all the menu items and pulls out basic numbers — how much each
  dish costs to make, which ingredients are used most often, and what
  equipment is needed. This is just background information used in the report.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — Collect the food cost of every dish on the menu.
  Step 2 — Find the most expensive 5 dishes and the cheapest 5 dishes.
  Step 3 — Count which ingredients appear most across all dishes.
  Step 4 — List all the equipment needed (grill, fryer, etc.).
  Step 5 — Show which dishes are scheduled on which day of the week.

FORMULA:
  Average cost = add up all dish costs, then divide by the number of dishes
  Middle cost  = line all costs from low to high and pick the one in the middle

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  Like a chef looking at next week's menu and writing down:
  "We use chicken the most. Our most expensive dish is the steak burger.
   We need the grill every day."

================================================================================
AGENT 3 — Playbook Check
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Checks if the menu is following the official rules set in the Grill Station
  Playbook. The playbook says how many burgers, vegan options, fries, and
  special daily items should appear each day.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — Read every dish name and put it into one of these groups:
    • SIDE     — toppings, sauces, or garnishes (e.g. "sliced tomato",
                 "shredded lettuce", "pickle"). These are not counted.
    • VEGAN    — plant-based dishes (e.g. "black bean burger",
                 "veggie burger"). Checked first.
    • BURGER   — beef burgers (e.g. "cheeseburger", "smash burger").
    • FRIES    — fries or potato sides (e.g. "french fries", "waffle fries").
    • DAILY FEATURE — everything else (rotating specials, sandwiches, etc.)

  Step 2 — Count how many dishes from each group appear each day.

  Step 3 — Compare the counts against the playbook limits:
    • Burger        → maximum 1 per day
    • Daily Feature → maximum 2 per day
    • Vegan         → minimum 1 per day (must have at least 1)
    • Fries         → minimum 1 per day (must have at least 1)

  Step 4 — If all counts are within the limits → PASS. Otherwise → FAIL.

FORMULA:
  VIOLATION (too many) → actual count is MORE than the allowed maximum
  VIOLATION (too few)  → actual count is LESS than the required minimum
  PASS = no violations found on any day

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  On Tuesday the menu has 2 beef burgers.
  The playbook says max 1 burger per day.
  Result → VIOLATION. Too many burgers on Tuesday.

================================================================================
AGENT 4 — Duplicate Checker
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Looks for mistakes in the menu data — specifically if the same dish was
  accidentally added to the same day more than once.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Check 1 — Same day, same meal time:
    Look at every meal period on every day. If the same dish appears twice
    in the same slot → that is a duplicate mistake.

  Check 2 — Same dish across different meal times:
    If a dish is listed under both Lunch AND Dinner on the same day
    (when it should only be in one) → that is also flagged.

FORMULA:
  DUPLICATE = same dish appearing 2 or more times in the same day/meal slot

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  "Cheeseburger" appears twice on Wednesday Lunch.
  This agent spots it and flags it as a data error.

================================================================================
AGENT 5 — Variety Score
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Gives the menu a variety score between 0 and 1. A high score means
  customers see many different dishes across the week. A low score means
  the same few dishes repeat too often.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — Set aside the sides, sauces, and toppings (they are the same
           every day and do not count toward variety).
  Step 2 — Count how many total dish-slots are filled across the week
           (e.g. a dish scheduled 5 days fills 5 slots).
  Step 3 — Count how many unique (different) main dishes exist.
  Step 4 — Divide unique dishes by total slots to get the score.
  Step 5 — Read the score:
    0.6 or above → Great variety. Customers see lots of different things.
    0.3 to 0.6   → Okay variety. Could add more rotation.
    Below 0.3    → Poor variety. The same dishes repeat too much.

FORMULA:
  Variety Score = Number of unique dishes ÷ Total dish-slots for the week

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  5 different main dishes each running across 8 slots = 5 ÷ 8 = 0.625 → Great
  2 different main dishes each running across 8 slots = 2 ÷ 8 = 0.250 → Poor

================================================================================
AGENT 6 — Repetition Tracker
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  For every single dish on the menu, counts exactly how many days it appears.
  Raises a flag if any main dish shows up on 4 or more days in the same week,
  because that is too repetitive for customers.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — Look at each dish and count the number of different days it is
           scheduled.
  Step 2 — If a main dish (not a side) appears on 4 or more days → flag it.
  Step 3 — Sort all dishes with the most-repeated ones at the top.

THRESHOLD:
  4 or more days in one week = HIGH REPETITION flag

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  "Cheeseburger" is on the menu Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday.
  That is 4 days → flagged as repeating too often.
  "Grilled Chicken Sandwich" appears only on Monday and Friday → fine.

================================================================================
AGENT 7 — Plant-Based Check
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Counts what percentage of the weekly menu is made up of plant-based
  (vegan/vegetarian) dishes. Aramark has pledged that at least 44% of
  all offerings should be plant-based. This agent checks whether the
  menu meets that promise.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — For each dish, check if it is plant-based.
    First look: does the dish name contain words like "vegan", "black bean",
    "tofu", "beyond", "impossible", "plant-based", or "veggie burger"?
    Second look: if the name does not say it, check the recipe instructions
    and ingredient list for plant-based clues.

  Step 2 — Skip toppings and condiments (cheese, tomato, ketchup, lettuce,
           mayo) so they don't unfairly boost the plant-based count.

  Step 3 — Add up the total number of dish slots for the week.
           Add up the number of those slots that are plant-based.

  Step 4 — Calculate the percentage and check if it hits 44%.

FORMULA:
  Plant-Based % = (Number of plant-based dish slots ÷ Total dish slots) × 100
  PASS = Plant-Based % is 44% or higher
  GAP  = 44% minus the actual % (how much more is needed)

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  10 total dish slots this week, 3 are plant-based.
  3 ÷ 10 × 100 = 30%.
  30% is below 44% → NOT compliant. Gap = 14%. Need more plant-based options.

================================================================================
AGENT 8 — Protein Balance Check
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Looks at the protein choices on the menu (beef vs. chicken, turkey, fish,
  or plant-based) and checks if beef appears too much. If beef dominates,
  it raises the food cost risk. The playbook says swapping just one beef
  dish per week for a non-beef option reduces the cost risk by 18%.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — Sort every dish into one of two groups:
    • BEEF       — dishes with beef, cheeseburger, smash burger, etc.
    • ALTERNATIVE — dishes with chicken, turkey, fish, black bean,
                    tofu, beyond meat, impossible burger, etc.
    (If a dish has zero scheduled days, it is skipped.)

  Step 2 — Count the total scheduled slots for beef and for alternatives.

  Step 3 — Decide the risk level:
    HIGH risk   → beef appears 5+ times AND alternatives appear 1 or fewer
    HIGH risk   → beef appears 3+ times AND alternatives appear fewer than 2
    MEDIUM risk → beef appears 3+ times BUT alternatives appear 2 or more
    LOW risk    → beef appears fewer than 3 times AND alternatives appear 2+

  Step 4 — Write a recommendation based on the risk level.

FORMULA:
  Beef appearing 3 or more days     = too much beef
  Alternatives appearing 2 or more  = good enough variety
  1 beef-to-alternative swap per week = 18% lower cost risk (per playbook)

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  Beef burger appears 4 days. Chicken sandwich appears only 1 day.
  → HIGH risk. Recommendation: swap one beef day for chicken or plant-based.

================================================================================
AGENT 9 — Report Writer
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Takes all the findings from the 6 checkers above and writes them up into
  one clean, easy-to-read report for managers. It uses an AI writing tool
  (Google Gemini) to put everything together in plain language. If the AI
  is not available, it falls back to a ready-made template.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Path A — With AI (Google Gemini):
    Step 1 — Give Gemini all the findings: what passed, what failed, variety
             score, repetition flags, plant-based %, protein balance.
    Step 2 — Ask Gemini to write a structured report with:
             • Overall status (pass or fail)
             • What rules were broken and why
             • Rotation and repetition issues
             • Plant-based gap (if any)
             • Numbered recommendations with playbook references
    Step 3 — If Gemini's reply is too short or something goes wrong,
             switch to Path B.

  Path B — Without AI (backup template):
    Step 1 — Loop through each failed check and write a plain description.
    Step 2 — Add plant-based gap if below 44%.
    Step 3 — List any high-repetition dishes.
    Step 4 — Print numbered recommendations with exact playbook quotes.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  Think of this agent as the manager who collects all the checkers' notes
  and writes one final summary email to send to the team.

================================================================================
AGENT 10 — Full Day Summary
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
  Takes the three separate reports (one for Breakfast, one for Lunch, one
  for Dinner) and combines them into a single summary for the whole day.
  This is the final big-picture view for senior managers.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Step 1 — Receive the three meal-period reports as input.
  Step 2 — Ask Google Gemini AI to combine them into one overall report.
  Step 3 — The combined report includes:
            • A short 2-3 sentence summary of the whole day
            • How well the playbook was followed across all meals
            • Variety and rotation patterns for the full day
            • Cost and nutrition highlights
            • 3-5 clear action steps for improvement
            • Total length kept under 800 words (easy to read)
  Step 4 — Clean up the output and send back the final report.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
  Breakfast report says vegan is missing.
  Lunch report says too many beef burgers.
  Dinner report says great variety.
  This agent reads all three and writes:
  "Today the station struggled with protein balance at lunch and missed vegan
   requirements at breakfast, but dinner rotation was excellent.
   Recommended actions: ..."
